Senehi et al. (2021). Prevention Science. PMC12053350. N=127 parent-child dyads. Child age M=18.38 months (range 5-44 months).

Study design: Parental ACEs measured via ACE questionnaire. Child stress measured via hair cortisol concentration (HCC — long-term physiological stress marker). Parental emotional availability (EA) measured via structured observation.

Key findings:
- Parental ACE → child HCC: significant positive association (β=.63, p<.05; quadratic — stronger at higher ACE levels)
- Overall model explained 26% of variance in child HCC
- EA MODERATION: parental EA moderates the ACE→HCC pathway
  - Lower EA parents: ACE effects increase with more adversity (B=0.07, p<.05) — intergenerational transmission
  - Higher EA parents: ACE effects DECREASE with more adversity (B=-0.09, p<.05) — buffering/protection
  - Effect size: Hedges' g=0.29 (small-medium)

Mechanism: Emotionally available parenting interrupts the physiological transmission of parental trauma to children. Even parents with high ACE can break the cycle by being emotionally present.

Key concept: "부모 ACE↑ → 아이 코르티솔↑, 그러나 부모의 정서적 가용성(EA)↑이면 이 전달 역전 → '거기 있어주기'가 세대간 트라우마 전이를 끊는 메커니즘"
