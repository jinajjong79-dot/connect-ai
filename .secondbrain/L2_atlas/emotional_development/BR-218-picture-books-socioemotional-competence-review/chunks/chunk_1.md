Title: Shared Book Reading and Social-Emotional Competences in Educational Settings: A Narrative Review
PMC ID: PMC12395914
Year: 2025
Tier: 2 | Grade: A ★
Category: emotional_development
Keywords: picture books, shared book reading, social-emotional competence, emotion understanding, prosocial behavior, empathy, emotion vocabulary, preschool, toddler, narrative review

---

ABSTRACT

This narrative review examines 15 empirical studies (5 nursery, 10 preschool settings) investigating how shared book reading (SBR) interventions promote children's social-emotional competence (SEC) in ages 1.5-6 years. Key finding: SBR combined with structured emotion discourse is "a powerful mechanism for fostering social and emotional competence among young children."

Core finding: "그림책→감정어휘↑ 감정이해↑ 친사회행동↑. 만4세가 가장 큰 효과."

INTRODUCTION

Why shared book reading for social-emotional competence:

Picture books offer a unique developmental context:
1. Emotional distance: Children can process difficult emotions through characters ("그 아이가 무서웠나봐요") before directly owning the emotion
2. Adult-guided emotion discourse: Reading creates a natural context for naming, explaining, and discussing emotions
3. Theory of mind scaffolding: "What does the character feel? Why?" → perspective-taking development
4. Emotional vocabulary exposure: Stories naturally embed emotion words in narrative context
5. Repetition: Favorite books read multiple times = multiple exposures to same emotion scenarios

Theoretical grounding:
- Vygotsky: Dyadic learning — adult scaffolds child's emotional understanding through shared activity
- Emotion socialization (Gottman framework):
  - Modeling: Adult demonstrates emotional responses to story
  - Contingency: Adult responds to child's emotional reactions during reading
  - Teaching (direct): Adult explicitly explains character emotions
- Bruner's dual landscape: Stories contain both action sequence AND psychological dimension (what characters feel/think)

The dual landscape is key: Children must learn to track BOTH what happens AND how characters feel. SBR teaches this simultaneously.

METHODS

Design: Narrative systematic review
Studies included: 15 empirical studies
Age range: 1.5-6 years (toddlers through preschool)
Settings: 5 nursery (1.5-3 years), 10 preschool (3-6 years)
Populations: Diverse — low-SES, Roma children, Arab preschoolers, mainstream Western
Intervention types: Structured SBR programs, teacher-led emotion discussion, parent-child reading

RESULTS

Overview of effects (15 studies):
- All 15 studies reported positive effects of SBR on at least one SEC outcome
- Effects persisted at follow-up in studies that measured long-term outcomes
- Benefits observed across diverse cultural populations

Specific outcome domains:

1. Emotion Vocabulary:
- SBR + emotion discussion → significant increases in emotion word production and comprehension
- Effect larger when adults explicitly labeled emotions during reading ("The bear is angry — see his face? His fists are clenched")
- Age effect: 4-year-olds showed largest vocabulary gains (sensitive period for emotion labeling)
- "만4세가 가장 큰 효과" for emotion vocabulary

2. Emotion Understanding:
- Toddlers (1.5-3): improved emotion recognition from facial expressions after SBR
- Preschoolers (3-6): improved situational emotion understanding ("why does the character feel sad?")
- Theory of mind scores improved after SBR programs targeting character mental states
- Key moderator: Books with psychologically complex characters (not just happy/sad binary) → greater theory of mind gains

3. Prosocial Behavior:
- SBR interventions → significant increases in sharing, helping, comforting peers
- Particularly strong for programs using books with explicit prosocial themes
- Mechanism: Children rehearse prosocial scenarios through story → generalize to classroom
- Effect consistent across low-SES and mainstream populations

4. Empathy:
- Books with first-person emotional narratives ("I felt scared when...") → greater empathy gains than third-person
- Children showed increased perspective-taking and emotional responsiveness to peers post-intervention
- Parent-child reading at home with emotion discussion → empathy development beyond classroom effects

Critical Implementation Components for Maximum Benefit:

1. Teacher/Parent Training (ESSENTIAL):
- Untrained adults: tend to read for comprehension ("what happened?")
- Trained adults: ask emotion-focused questions ("how did the character feel? Why?")
- Training gap: most parents and teachers read picture books without explicit emotion discourse
- Training duration: even 1-2 hour workshop significantly improves quality

2. Small-Group Settings:
- Dyad or small group (3-4 children): more adult-child emotional exchange
- Large group reading: less individual scaffolding, smaller SEC effects
- Ideal: 1 adult + 2-4 children for emotion-focused reading

3. Book Selection:
- Stories with: emotional content, psychological complexity, characters facing moral dilemmas
- Books that show: emotion causes, emotion consequences, emotion regulation attempts
- Avoid: books where characters have uniform positive outcomes (no emotional struggle)
- Good examples: books with conflict, mistakes, repair, and resolution

4. Structured Dialogue:
Before reading: "이 아이 표정을 봐. 어떤 기분일 것 같아?"
During reading: "지금 주인공은 어떤 기분일까? 왜 그럴까?"
After reading: "네가 그 아이였다면 어떤 기분이었을 것 같아?"

DISCUSSION

The mechanism pathway:
SBR → emotion word exposure → emotion labeling practice → emotion understanding → empathy → prosocial behavior

Why age 4 is peak sensitive period:
- Age 4: sufficient language to discuss characters' internal states
- Age 4: theory of mind developing rapidly (false belief task passing)
- Age 4: not yet emotionally defensive about complex emotions
- Age 4: high interest in narrative and character motivation
- "세현이 연령 직격" — this aligns directly with the channel's target child

Research gaps identified:
- Longer follow-up studies needed (most studies end at 3-6 months post-intervention)
- More focus on toddlerhood (1.5-3 years) needed
- Cross-cultural emotion socialization practices need more study
- Optimal session duration and group size remain unclear

For Korean parenting context:
- 한국 부모의 책 읽기: quantity tends to be high, but emotion-focused discussion quality often low
- Most parents ask "무슨 일이 일어났어?" (What happened?) not "주인공이 어떤 기분이었을까?" (How did the character feel?)
- Simple training for parents: shift questions from event-focused to emotion-focused
- Channel content idea: "그림책 읽을 때 이 한 마디만 추가해보세요"

CONCLUSION

Shared book reading combined with structured emotion discourse is a powerful, accessible, and cost-effective intervention for social-emotional competence development in children ages 1.5-6. Age 4 is the peak sensitive period for emotion vocabulary gains. Structured adult-guided emotion discussion is the critical active ingredient — not reading alone.

Practical implication: The same picture book read with emotion discussion vs. without produces measurably different outcomes. The investment is not in books but in the quality of adult-child dialogue around them.
