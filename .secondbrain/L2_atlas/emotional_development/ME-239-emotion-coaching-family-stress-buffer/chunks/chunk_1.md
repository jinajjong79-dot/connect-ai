Parental Emotion Coaching Moderates the Effects of Family Stress on Internalizing Symptoms in Middle Childhood and Adolescence
Authors: Frances M. Lobo, Erika Lunkenheimer, Rachel G. Lucas-Thompson, Natasha S. Seiter
Source: PMC9496639 | Study 1: N=153 ages 10-17; Study 2: N=82 ages 8-11.75
ATLAS ID: ME-239
Category: emotional_development / emotion coaching / stress resilience
Tier: 2 | Grade: A ★★
Year: 2022

=== OVERVIEW ===

Two-study investigation demonstrating that parental emotion coaching functions as a protective buffer against the negative effects of family stress on children's internalizing symptoms (depression, anxiety). When parents use emotion coaching, the link between family stress and child mental health problems is significantly weakened. This is the "감정코칭 = 스트레스 방탄막" evidence.

=== STUDY 1 ===

Sample: N=153 youth, ages 10-17
Measures:
- Maternal self-reports of emotion coaching (EC) philosophy
- Maternal self-reports of emotion dismissing (ED) philosophy
- Family chaos measure as stressor
- Youth internalizing symptoms: depression, anxiety (self and parent report)
Design: Cross-sectional moderation analysis

Results:
- "Positive associations between family stress and youth internalizing symptoms were ONLY present when parental EC philosophy was LOWER"
- Higher EC = no significant stress-to-symptom pathway
- Higher EC associated with NEGATIVE relationship between family stress and depressive symptoms
- EC promotes RESILIENCE: high-stress families with high EC showed better outcomes than expected

=== STUDY 2 ===

Sample: N=82 youth, ages 8-11.75
Measures:
- OBSERVED parental EC and ED behaviors (behavioral coding of family interactions)
- Chronic stressor assessments
- Youth internalizing symptoms
Design: Observational, cross-sectional moderation

Results:
- Replicated Study 1 findings with OBSERVED (not self-reported) behaviors
- "Family stress → internalizing symptoms" link was buffered by higher observed EC behaviors
- Emotion dismissing showed NO significant moderating effects in either study

=== KEY CONCLUSION ===

"When parents validate emotions and help youth navigate feelings constructively, youth appear better protected against stress's negative psychological effects."

EC buffered the stress-symptom link; ED did not independently worsen it.

=== THEORETICAL SIGNIFICANCE ===

1. MODERATION MECHANISM: EC does not prevent stressors — it changes how children PROCESS stressors
2. SELF-REPORT + OBSERVED convergence: findings replicate across measurement methods
3. Intervention implication: Teaching parents EC can function as a UNIVERSAL PREVENTION strategy for child mental health even in high-stress families
4. Emotion dismissing ≠ mirror image of coaching: ED has its own different (and less direct) effects

=== CLINICAL APPLICATION ===

High-stress families (poverty, marital conflict, chaos) are exactly the families that most need EC intervention. These findings argue for:
- Universal EC programs in preschool/school settings
- EC training for parents in high-adversity contexts
- EC as a mental health prevention strategy, not just a "nice to have"
