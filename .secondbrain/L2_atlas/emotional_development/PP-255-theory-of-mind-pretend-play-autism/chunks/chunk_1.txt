Chan et al. (2016). Hong Kong Journal of Occupational Therapy. PMC6091989. N=20 ASD children ages 3-7.11y. Clinical sample, small N — Grade B.

Study design: Cross-sectional. Theory of Mind (ToM) tested via false-belief tasks. Pretend play measured via standardized assessment (NOS = Number of Object Substitutions, NIA = Number of Imaginative Acts). Playfulness measured via ToP (Test of Playfulness). Autism severity via CARS.

Key findings:
- ToM → NOS (number of object substitutions): R²=.158, p=.002
- ToM → NIA (number of imaginative acts) inverse: R²=.175, p=.001
- CARS (autism severity) → internal control: R²=.125
- CARS → framing: R²=.071
- ToM deficit associated with pretend play performance but NOT with playfulness
- Conclusion: Metarepresentation (ToM) is a shared cognitive mechanism for both pretend play and ToM; autism affects pretend play via ToM pathway but preserves some playfulness dimensions

Key concept: "ToM deficit → pretend play performance deficit (metarepresentation as shared mechanism); but playfulness (motivation/engagement) preserved even in ASD"

Note: Small N=20 clinical sample, results should be interpreted with caution. Replicated pattern aligns with larger literature on metarepresentation.
