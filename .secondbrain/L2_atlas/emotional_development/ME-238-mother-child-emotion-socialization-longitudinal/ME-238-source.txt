Etch the Emotional Life: Mother-Child Emotion Socialization from Age 3 to 6 Years
Source: PMC11107839 | Longitudinal, N=349 dyads, ages 3→6
ATLAS ID: ME-238
Category: emotional_development / emotion socialization / longitudinal
Tier: 2 | Grade: A ★
Year: 2024

=== OVERVIEW ===

Longitudinal study of 349 mother-child dyads performing a cooperative etch-a-sketch task at three time points (ages 3, 4.5, and 6 years). Researchers coded emotional expression, coaching (supportive responses), and dismissing (unsupportive responses) behaviors during 30-second intervals. Novel finding: children are active participants in emotion socialization, not passive recipients. Emotion socialization is bidirectional.

=== DESIGN ===

Sample: N=349 mother-child dyads
Method: Cooperative etch-a-sketch task (30-second interval behavioral coding)
Time points: Ages 3, 4.5, and 6 years (3 assessments)
Coded behaviors:
- Emotional expression (positive and negative)
- Coaching: supportive responses to partner's emotions
- Dismissing: unsupportive/minimizing responses to partner's emotions

=== DEVELOPMENTAL TRAJECTORIES ===

Mother trajectories:
- Decreased positive emotional expression over time (unexpected)
- Negative emotion expression remained stable
- Decreased coaching of positive emotions
- Coaching of negative emotions stayed constant
- Decreased dismissing of emotions over time

Child trajectories:
- Decreased positive emotional expression over time (parallels mothers)
- Negative emotion expression remained stable
- Increased coaching of mothers' positive emotions (children became active coaches)
- Decreased dismissing of emotions over time

=== KEY FINDINGS (Within-Dyad Associations) ===

Mother coaching → Child expression:
- "Mothers' time-varying coaching of emotion, whether positive or negative, was positively related to children's expression of same-valence emotion"
- When mothers coach more, children express more — in the SAME emotional valence

Child coaching → Mother expression:
- "Children's time-varying coaching of emotion, whether positive or negative, was positively related to mothers' expression of valence-matching emotion"
- Children's coaching predicts mothers' expression — the relationship is truly bidirectional

Dismissing effects:
- Mothers' increases in dismissing also related to children's increased expression of matching emotions (complex relationship)
- Children's dismissing of mothers' positive emotions: no significant association with mothers' expressions

=== THEORETICAL SIGNIFICANCE ===

Bidirectional model: Challenges traditional ONE-DIRECTIONAL model of parental influence on children. Children become increasingly active participants in emotion socialization from ages 3-6.

Transactional development: Emotion socialization is a dynamic, changing process — not a fixed parenting behavior applied to a passive child. By age 6, children can actively coach parents.

Implications for intervention: Targeting ONLY parent behavior may be insufficient. As children develop, targeting the child-parent interaction system may be more effective.
