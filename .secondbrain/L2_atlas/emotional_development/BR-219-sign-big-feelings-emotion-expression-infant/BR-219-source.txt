Title: The Sign 4 Big Feelings Intervention: Improving Early Language Development and Emotional Well-Being in Preschool Children Using Sign Language
PMC ID: PMC9166658
Year: 2022
Tier: 2 | Grade: B
Category: emotional_development
Keywords: sign language, emotion expression, preschool, early language, wellbeing, gestural communication, emotion vocabulary, EAL children, disadvantaged

---

ABSTRACT

This study evaluated the Sign 4 Big Feelings intervention — a sign language program designed to improve early language development and emotional well-being in preschool children in Luton, UK. N=111 children (mean age 39 months), 54% boys, 33% with English as additional language (EAL), 3-month intervention.

Key Findings:
- Words understood: mean 6.3 → 11.4 (significant increase)
- Words spoken: mean 4.1 → 9.6 (significant increase)
- Wellbeing (Leuven scale): significant improvement
- All 4 early years outcome domains: substantial gains
- Effect sizes: Cohen's d = 1.17–1.56 (LARGE)
- EAL children: ~4 steps progress vs. ~2 steps for native speakers (greater benefit)

Core message: "몸짓+감정명명 결합 개입→언어 아직 미숙한 유아도 감정 표현 가능"

INTRODUCTION

The communication gap problem:
Children ages 2-4 have strong emotional experiences but often lack the verbal language to express them. This gap between what children feel and what they can say contributes to:
- Frustration-driven behavior problems ("tantrum = communication failure")
- Emotion regulation difficulties
- Social withdrawal in non-native language environments
- Reduced wellbeing from inability to communicate needs

Sign language as bridge:
Baby signing and Makaton (simplified sign language for language development) have decades of evidence showing:
- Children develop gestural communication before verbal
- Signs can be learned faster than spoken words
- Gesture + word pairing accelerates spoken language acquisition
- Emotional gesture-word pairs may be particularly powerful (body already carries emotion)

Sign 4 Big Feelings program:
- Uses storybooks + dolls depicting various emotions
- 16 key emotion-related words taught with simple sign language
- Integrated into regular storytimes by practitioners
- Emotions targeted: happy, sad, angry, scared, worried, surprised, disgusted, proud, embarrassed, jealous, excited, calm, frustrated, shy, loving, lonely
- "몸짓+감정명명" — gestural + verbal pairing for emotion expression

METHODS

Design: Pre-post intervention (no control group)
Sample: N=111 children
Mean age: 39 months (range ~24-60 months)
Gender: 54% boys, 46% girls
Language: 33% English as Additional Language (EAL)
Funded status: Proportion receiving free early years (disadvantaged indicator)
Setting: Preschool settings in Luton, UK
Duration: 3 months

Intervention delivery:
- Practitioners received training in Sign 4 Big Feelings
- Integrated into existing storytime routines (not additional sessions)
- Storybooks featuring the 16 emotion signs
- Emotion dolls with facial expressions for demonstration
- Signs used consistently throughout the day when emotion words arose

Measures:
- Language (words understood and spoken): practitioner-rated observation scale
- Wellbeing: Leuven Scale of Well-Being (observation-based)
- Early years outcomes: 4 domain assessment (communication/language, personal/social/emotional, physical, literacy/maths)

RESULTS

Language outcomes:
- Words understood: 6.3 → 11.4 (increase of 5.1 words, significant p<.001)
- Words spoken: 4.1 → 9.6 (increase of 5.5 words, significant p<.001)
- Effect sizes: d = 1.17–1.56 (LARGE — unusually high for language interventions)

Wellbeing:
- Leuven well-being score: significant improvement across all children
- Trend: children who gained most in language showed greatest wellbeing improvement

Early years outcomes (all 4 domains significant):
- Personal, Social and Emotional Development
- Communication and Language
- Physical Development
- Literacy and Mathematics

Group differences — EAL children:
- EAL children: ~4 steps progress on early years outcomes
- Native English children: ~2 steps progress
- EAL children benefited MORE — sign language provided language-neutral emotion expression channel
- Gestural communication bypasses language barrier for emotion expression

Group differences — disadvantaged children:
- Funded status children: 4.15 steps progress
- Non-funded children: 2.5 steps progress
- Disadvantaged children benefited MORE — sign language gave extra expressive channel where verbal was limited

Gender differences: NONE — no significant gender effects across any measure

DISCUSSION

Why gestural emotion training works:
1. Motor-verbal pairing: Using the body (hand sign) + voice (word) simultaneously creates multi-modal memory trace
2. Body-first pathway: Signs are produced by the body → activates proprioceptive-interoceptive system → connects to IC-208/209's body-emotion link
3. Slower communication: Signs force the pace slightly slower than speech → creates pause that supports emotional processing
4. Social reinforcement: Adults respond to both sign and word → double positive reinforcement
5. Language-neutral: Works before verbal fluency, works across languages

The frustration-expression bridge:
A 2-year-old who SIGNS "angry" instead of hitting has:
- Successfully communicated the emotion
- Received a verbal response (adult says "angry! You're angry!")
- Bypassed the violent behavior
- Practiced emotion labeling in the body

This is the earliest form of Gottman's Step 4 (labeling) — before words are fluent.

EAL children benefit most — why this matters for Korean families:
Korean immigrant families in English-speaking countries, or Korean children in international environments, may benefit from gestural emotion bridging. But even in Korean monolingual contexts:
- Very young children (18-36 months) are pre-verbal even in their native language
- Signing "슬퍼" at 18 months is possible before saying it
- The body-gesture pairing accelerates verbal acquisition

Korean parenting application:
Simple parent implementation:
- 4-6 basic signs (행복/슬픔/화남/무서움/피곤/아파)
- Pair sign with word always: [sign 슬픔] + "슬프구나"
- Use dolls or stuffed animals with emotion faces for demonstration
- Read picture books with emotion signs throughout

Limitations:
- No control group (cannot rule out maturation effects)
- 3-month duration (cannot establish long-term retention)
- Small sample for subgroup analyses
- Practitioner fidelity varied
- Leuven scale has limited validation for this age group
- Restricted wellbeing data collection

CONCLUSION

The Sign 4 Big Feelings intervention produced large effect size improvements in emotion vocabulary, language development, and wellbeing in children as young as 39 months. Gestural + verbal emotion pairing is particularly effective for children with limited verbal capacity (EAL, disadvantaged, very young). It provides a pathway for emotion expression before verbal fluency is established, preventing the frustration-behavior cycle.

ATLAS connections:
- BR-218: picture books as primary vehicle (Sign 4 uses books + dolls)

- Gottman 감정코칭 Step 4 (이름붙이기): gestural pairing as earliest form
