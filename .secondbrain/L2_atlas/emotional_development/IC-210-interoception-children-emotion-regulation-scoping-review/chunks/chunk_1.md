ABSTRACT

Background: During the primary school years, children develop skills and body functions which support their occupational participation in activities like play, self-care, academic learning, social participation, and sleep.

Objective: To explore what is known in the published and grey literature about the relationship between interoception and emotional regulation or anxiety, and occupational participation in school-aged children.

Methods: Scoping review with narrative synthesis. Databases searched (June 2, 2025): MEDLINE, EMBASE, PsycINFO, AMED, Scopus, CINAHL, Nursing and Allied Health Database, ERIC, Google Scholar. Target population: primary school children aged 5-13 years. Evidence critically appraised using the Crowe Critical Appraisal Tool.

Results: Two themes emerged with capacity to impact childhood occupational participation:
1. Interoception and emotional regulation may be related
2. Education may improve interoceptive awareness

No anxiety-related themes were identified in the included evidence.

Conclusion: This review supports the need for further research to understand the relationship between children's interoception, their ability to regulate emotions and anxiety, and their impact on childhood occupational participation.

Keywords: anxiety, emotional regulation, interoception, occupational participation, school-aged child

EXTENDED SUMMARY (from search results and context)

Conceptual Framework:
Interoception is the perception of internal bodily states — hunger, thirst, heart rate, breathing, pain, temperature, muscle tension. In school-aged children, interoceptive awareness underlies:

- PLAY: Noticing fatigue, excitement, overstimulation — allows self-regulation during play
- SELF-CARE: Recognizing hunger, toileting needs, pain — enables independent self-management
- ACADEMIC LEARNING: Attention regulation, noticing anxiety/stress — supports learning readiness
- SOCIAL PARTICIPATION: Reading one's own emotional states → social reciprocity
- SLEEP: Noticing tiredness, pre-sleep relaxation — sleep hygiene

Two Core Themes from Review:

Theme 1: Interoception and Emotional Regulation Are Related
- Children with better interoceptive awareness show more adaptive emotion regulation
- Poor interoceptive awareness → difficulty identifying emotions (alexithymia-like pattern)
- Emotional dysregulation episodes in school often preceded by missed interoceptive signals
- "감정조절 어려움을 가진 아동은 몸의 신호를 놓치는 경향이 있다"

Theme 2: Education Can Improve Interoceptive Awareness
- Structured interoception programs (e.g., The Interoception Curriculum) show measurable gains
- Even brief educational interventions (8-10 weeks) improve body signal recognition
- Teacher/parent-led activities can scaffold interoceptive development
- Occupational therapy context: interoception-informed sensory diet approaches

No Anxiety Themes Found:
- Insufficient evidence to conclude direct interoception-anxiety relationship in school-aged children
- Further research needed — likely relationship exists but not yet well-studied

Occupational Therapy Framework (OTPF-4):
- All occupations of childhood (play, ADL, education, rest, social participation) require some degree of interoceptive competence
- Interventions targeting interoception may have occupational performance effects beyond just emotional regulation
- School-based OT has emerging role in interoception education

Implications for Practice:
- Classroom sensory check-ins ("What does your body need right now?")
- Play-based interoceptive activities embedded in daily routine
- Parent education on noticing and narrating body states with children
- "Feeling thermometers" combined with body maps more effective than emotion labels alone
