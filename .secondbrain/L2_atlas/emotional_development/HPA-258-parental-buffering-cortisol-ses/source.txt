Parenteau et al. (2020). Developmental Psychopathology. PMC9306352. N=180 children ages 9-11 years (M=9.9).

Note: User-reported age range was 4-7, but actual study participants were 9-11 years old.

Study design: TSST (Trier Social Stress Task) social-evaluative stressor. Conditions: with parent / with stranger / alone. Cortisol measured via salivary samples.

Key findings:
- Main effect: higher cortisol reactivity in stress vs. control: F(2,174)=19.85, p<.001
- Parental education × condition interaction: F(2,169)=3.93, p=.02
- Lower-education parents: BUFFERED cortisol (parent condition M=0.08 vs. alone M=0.26)
- Higher-education parents: AMPLIFIED cortisol (parent condition M=0.25 vs. alone M=0.15)
- Within parent condition: state anxiety correlated with cortisol r=.30, p=.03

Mechanism: Higher-education parents may emphasize performance/evaluation, increasing child anxiety; lower-education parents provide more direct comfort/support.

Key concept: "Social buffering (parent presence = stress buffer) is real BUT moderated by SES/parental education — more educated parents may amplify rather than buffer during performance tasks"
