# Parental Self-Perception, Parental Investment, and Early Childhood Developmental Outcomes: Evidence From Rural China

**Source:** Frontiers in Public Health, Volume 10, 2022. DOI: 10.3389/fpubh.2022.820113
**Authors:** Lei Wang, Ting Wang, Hui Li (Shaanxi Normal University); Kaiwen Guo (Shanghai Pinghe School); Lynn Hu (Pardee RAND Graduate School); Siqi Zhang, Scott Rozelle (Stanford University)
**PMC:** PMC9008586

## Abstract

Using a three-wave longitudinal survey conducted in 815 households in rural Western China, this study examines the association between parental self-perception and early childhood development and the mediation effect of parental investment on the association between parental self-perception and child development when sample children are at different ages in early childhood (18–30, 22–36, and 49–65 months).

Results demonstrate that parental self-perception is positively and significantly associated with child social-emotional development in all three age groups (18 to 65 months). Positive and significant association between parental self-perception and child cognitive development is found from ages 22 to 65 months. Parental investment plays a mediating role in the association between parental self-perception and child cognitive development.

## Key Findings

- Parental self-perception (self-efficacy) significantly predicts both cognitive and social-emotional outcomes from 18 to 65 months
- Parental investment (reading, singing, interactive play) mediates the relationship between self-perception and cognitive outcomes
- In rural China, ~45% of children under 5 show cognitive delays; ~36% show social-emotional delays
- Meta-analysis across 46 studies confirms strong positive association between parental investment and child outcomes in math, reading, and language
- Parents with higher self-efficacy demonstrate more engaged parenting practices

## Implications for Parent Education

- Strengthening parental self-perception is a leverage point for child development outcomes
- Parental investment activities (active play, reading together, singing) are key mediating behaviors
- Policy and education programs should target parental confidence alongside skill-building
- Early intervention (18–30 months) is critical window for establishing parental self-perception patterns
- Social-emotional development benefits persist across the full early childhood period (18–65 months)
