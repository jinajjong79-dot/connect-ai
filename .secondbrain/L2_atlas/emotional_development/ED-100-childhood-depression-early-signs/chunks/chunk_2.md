3. MONITOR SPECIFIC HIGH-RISK PERIODS
   - Starting preschool/kindergarten (adjustment ≠ depression, but monitor)
   - After significant loss or family change
   - When parent is experiencing depression (increased monitoring essential)

4. TRUST YOUR INSTINCTS
   - Parents who sense "something is wrong" with their child's emotional state are often right
   - Persistent behavioral change + loss of joy + self-criticism = seek professional evaluation

5. EARLY INTERVENTION IS MORE EFFECTIVE
   - Brain neuroplasticity is highest in early childhood
   - Earlier treatment produces more durable outcomes
   - No benefit to "waiting to see" when clear warning signs are present

=== TREATMENT APPROACHES ===

Evidence-based interventions for preschool depression:

1. Parent-Child Interaction Therapy — Emotional Development (PCIT-ED)
   - Modified from established PCIT (used for behavior problems)
   - Focuses on strengthening emotional attunement in parent-child relationship
   - Teaches parents to enhance emotion development through interaction
   - Open trial results: "very promising"

2. Parent psychotherapy
   - Treating maternal depression reduces child depression risk
   - Addressing parent-child relationship quality

3. Child-Parent Psychotherapy (CPP)
   - For children who have experienced trauma
   - Focuses on parent-child relationship as vehicle for healing

NOTE: SSRIs (antidepressants) are generally NOT recommended as first-line for preschool depression due to developmental concerns. Relationship-based interventions are preferred.

=== KEY CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS ===

"The importance of identifying depression at the earliest possible point in development cannot be overstated."

"Preschool depression has been shown to display homotypic continuity with later childhood depression — depressed preschoolers are much more likely to have depression at school age."

"Early alterations in emotional development skills and capacities are key to the risk for depression — making emotion development support both prevention and treatment."
