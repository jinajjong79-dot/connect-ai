Title: Subjective Interoception and Alexithymia: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
PMC ID: PMC11542822
Year: 2024
Tier: 2 | Grade: A

Keywords: interoception, alexithymia, meta-analysis, systematic review, emotion identification, body awareness, MAIA, TAS-20, emotion processing

ABSTRACT

This systematic review and meta-analysis examined how self-reported interoception relates to alexithymia (difficulty identifying and describing emotions) across 32 cross-sectional studies published between 1996-2023.

Key Finding: "감정을 못 읽는 건 몸을 못 느끼기 때문일 수 있다." Different interoceptive dimensions relate to alexithymia differently — interoceptive confusion correlates strongly positively with alexithymia (r=0.57), while adaptive interoceptive beliefs correlate negatively (r=-0.30).

INTRODUCTION

Alexithymia (from Greek: a=lack, lexis=words, thymos=emotion):
- Difficulty identifying feelings
- Difficulty describing feelings to others
- Externally oriented thinking (preference for external over internal events)
- Estimated prevalence: 10% general population; up to 85% autistic individuals; elevated in PTSD, eating disorders, depression

The interoception-alexithymia link:
Hypothesis: Alexithymia is fundamentally an interoceptive processing problem. If you cannot accurately perceive and interpret bodily signals, you cannot reliably identify emotions (because emotions begin as bodily states).

"감정표현곤란증(알렉시시미아)이 있는 사람은 감정을 '모르는' 것이 아니라, 몸이 보내는 신호를 '못 읽는' 것이다."

Key interoceptive scales used in research:
- MAIA (Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness): 8 subscales including body listening, trusting, noticing, not-distracting, not-worrying, attention regulation, emotional awareness, self-regulation
- TAS-20 (Toronto Alexithymia Scale): difficulty identifying feelings, difficulty describing feelings, externally oriented thinking
- Heartbeat counting task (objective IAc): ability to accurately count own heartbeats

METHODS

Design: Systematic review and meta-analysis
Search: PsycINFO, PubMed, Web of Science
Included: 32 cross-sectional studies (1996-2023)
Population: Mostly non-clinical university samples; some clinical
Countries: Predominantly Western (UK, USA, Australia, Germany)
Assessment of bias: STROBE criteria

RESULTS

Association 1: Interoceptive Confusion → Alexithymia (POSITIVE, STRONG)
- Measure: Scales assessing confusion about body sensations, feeling overwhelmed by sensations
- Effect size: r = 0.57 (large positive correlation)
- Interpretation: People who feel confused by bodily signals → more likely to struggle identifying emotions
- "몸에서 뭔가 느껴지는데 그게 뭔지 모르겠다 = 감정표현곤란 위험"

Association 2: Autonomic Reactivity → Alexithymia (POSITIVE, MODERATE)
- Higher attention to autonomic signals (heart pounding, breathing changes) correlated with alexithymia
- Paradox: This means people with alexithymia DO notice some body signals but cannot interpret them
- They experience physical arousal but can't connect it to emotional states

Association 3: Adaptive Interoceptive Beliefs (MAIA) → Alexithymia (NEGATIVE, MODERATE)
- MAIA subscales: body listening, trusting, self-regulation
- r = -0.30 (moderate negative correlation)
- Adaptive beliefs = "I trust my body's signals" → lower alexithymia
- Self-regulation belief = "I can use body awareness to regulate" → lower alexithymia
- "몸을 믿고 잘 활용한다고 생각하는 사람들은 감정을 더 잘 인식한다"

Association 4: Interoceptive Accuracy Beliefs → Alexithymia (NEGATIVE)
- Believing one is accurate at detecting body signals → lower alexithymia
- Note: Belief in accuracy ≠ actual accuracy (heartbeat task)
- Subjective confidence in body reading protects against alexithymia

Key methodological finding:
"Different interoceptive self-report scales relate to alexithymia differentially"
- Confusion subscales: positive association (higher confusion = more alexithymia)
- Adaptive belief subscales: negative association (more adaptive = less alexithymia)
- Simply asking "how aware are you of your body?" is insufficient — dimension matters

DISCUSSION

The Confusion vs. Adaptive Pattern:
Two types of interoceptive difficulty:
1. Cannot perceive signals at all (low awareness) → less connected to alexithymia than expected
2. Perceives signals but cannot interpret/trust them (confusion) → strongly connected to alexithymia

This reframes alexithymia: It may not be about absent interoception but about CONFUSED or DISTRUSTED interoception. People with alexithymia may notice body sensations but cannot map them to emotional categories or trust their interpretations.

Developmental Implications for Children:
- Children need BOTH: (1) ability to notice body signals AND (2) frameworks to interpret them correctly
- "Your heart is pounding and your hands are sweaty — what does that mean?" needs scaffolding
- Parents who validate and name body sensations → children develop adaptive interoceptive beliefs
- Parents who dismiss ("you're fine, stop being dramatic") → children learn to distrust body signals → confusion pattern → alexithymia risk

Gottman감정코칭 Connection:
감정코칭 Step 1: 감정 인식 (emotion awareness) works through interoceptive pathway:
- Parent notices child's body language (body as external interoceptive signal)
- Names the signal: "I see your fists are clenched and you're breathing fast"
- Connects to emotion: "Sounds like you might be angry"
- This scaffolds the child's own interoceptive-to-emotion mapping

When this scaffolding is absent:
- Child notices confused body state but cannot label it
- Default response: acting out, dissociation, or somatization
- Alexithymia pattern can develop early

Protective factor: Interoceptive trust
- "My body gives me useful information" belief
- Parents cultivate this by treating body signals as valid and informative
- Not dismissing "my tummy hurts" as "you're just nervous"
- Exploring body signals with curiosity rather than dismissal

CONCLUSION

Interoceptive confusion — not absence — is the primary interoceptive correlate of alexithymia. Adaptive interoceptive beliefs (body trust, self-regulation through body awareness) protect against alexithymia. Interventions targeting confusion reduction and adaptive belief development may be more effective than generic body awareness training.
