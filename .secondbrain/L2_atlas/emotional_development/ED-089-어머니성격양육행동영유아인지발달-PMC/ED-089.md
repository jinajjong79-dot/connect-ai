## Key Concept: 부모 성격 마스크 (Personality Mask / Persona)

**핵심 개념:** "부모 성격 마스크"란 어머니의 실제 내면 성격(true personality)과 양육 역할 수행 시 사회적 기대에 맞게 표출되는 성격(parenting-mode personality) 사이의 차이를 의미한다. Carl Jung의 페르소나(persona) 이론에 근거하며, 어머니가 사회적 역할("좋은 어머니")을 수행하기 위해 의식적·무의식적으로 형성하는 성격 구조물이다.

**경로:** 어머니 성격 마스크 → 양육 행동 → 영유아 인지·사회·정서 발달

---

## Abstract

This study developed and validated an instrument assessing "personality masks of mothers of young children," addressing a gap in developmental psychology. Using mixed-methods grounded in Carl Jung's persona theory, researchers collected data from **486 mothers** in two-parent families in China's Z region across three phases:

1. **Scale development** via open-ended questionnaires — identifying three-dimensional structure: Collective Ideals, Personal Ideals, Physical/Mental Qualities
2. **Confirmatory factor analysis** — validating model with excellent fit indices
3. **Implicit memory experiment** — exploring unconscious expression

**Results:** Significant correlations emerged between mask dimensions and parenting behaviors, accounting for notable variance in parenting outcomes (η² = 0.123, explaining 12.3% of variance). The personality mask construct proved valuable for understanding maternal behavior. The study provides practical tools for research and intervention while highlighting the need for greater cultural diversity and ecological validity studies.

## 1. Introduction

### Theoretical Framework

Early childhood development profoundly influences subsequent physical and mental growth. As primary caregivers, mothers' parenting behaviors significantly impact children's social adjustment, emotional regulation, and cognitive development, with personality traits serving as the core factor shaping parenting practices.

Jung conceptualized the "persona" — a widely adopted psychological term referring to "dynamic expressive structures shaped by societal expectations, which differ from an individual's intrinsic traits." Subsequent scholars expanded this framework:

- **Guo (2012) and Samuels (2015):** Persona as composite representation integrating cultural norms, group consensus, and individual needs
- **Jacobi (1976), Fan (2008), and Markus & Kitayama (1991):** Cultural backgrounds influence persona's constituent dimensions

### Maternal Persona Definition

Regarding preschool mothers, Belsky (1984) and Fan (2008) proposed that maternal persona constitutes "a dynamic combination of traits actively constructed to align with social norms and family responsibilities." This represents dynamic equilibrium between:
- Personal characteristics
- Family environment
- Sociocultural influences

Mothers adjust behaviors according to "good mother" expectations, integrate self-growth needs, and maintain stable physical/mental states as foundation for consistent expression, forming a three-dimensional construct:

1. **Collective Ideals** (집단적 이상) — alignment with social/family norms
2. **Individual Ideals** (개인적 이상) — self-growth, confidence, achievement
3. **Physical-Mental Qualities** (신체·정신적 자질) — health, resilience, stability

### Research Gap

"Research on parenting by mothers of young children generally focuses on mothers' parenting behaviors, while neglecting the underlying psychological factors." An evaluation tool assessing mothers' collective unconscious and conscious levels is needed.

### Research Questions

1. What is the structure of personality masks in mothers of young children?
2. What are the manifestations of personality masks in mothers of young children?
3. What is the relationship between mothers' personality masks and their parenting behaviors?

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Research Design

Sequential mixed-methods design across three phases:

- **Phase 1:** Develop assessment tool via open-ended questionnaires and factor analysis
- **Phase 2:** Validate unconscious expression using implicit memory experiments
- **Phase 3:** Examine persona–parenting behavior associations via correlation analysis

### 2.2 Participants

#### Total Sample
- **Total N = 486 mothers** of preschool children in Region Z, China
- All from two-parent families
- Children aged 3–6 years, enrolled in regular kindergartens
- Multi-stage recruitment from regional kindergartens

#### Age Distribution (across phases)
- ≤25 years: 13.7–18.0%
- 26–35 years: 53.6–56%
- ≥36 years: 27.5–32.7%
- Urban residents: 82%

#### Phase 1 Participants (Lexical Construction & Factor Analysis)
- **Vocabulary Construction:** N = 153 valid questionnaires (200 recruited from Kindergarten H)
- **Factor Analysis:** N = 233 mothers (from Kindergarten J)

#### Phase 2 Participants (Implicit Memory Experiment)
- **N = 100 mothers** (from Kindergarten X)

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Biological mothers with children aged 3–6 in regular kindergartens
- Two-parent households (living with spouse)
- Resided in Region Z ≥2 years
- Basic Chinese literacy

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Children with diagnosed developmental disorders (autism, ADHD)
- Mothers with severe mental health issues (SCL-90 score >160)
- Single-parent, blended, or grandparent-led families
- Questionnaire response time <5 minutes or patterned responding
- Implicit memory experiment error rate >30% or abnormal reaction times (±3 SD)

#### Phase 3 Participants (Parenting Behavior Assessment)
- Same 100 mothers as Phase 2
- Completed immediately after implicit memory experiment

### 2.3 Procedure

#### Phase 1: Scale Development

**Step 1 — Open-Ended Questionnaires:**
- 200 mothers provided descriptions of "good mother" behaviors
- 102 initial statements generated via text analysis

**Step 2 — Dual-Track Expert Review:**
- Review panel: 4 experts + 5 mothers (Delphi method)
- Selection criteria: Statements scoring 10–13 points (≥75% expert agreement, ≥10 mother points)
- Result: 45 statements retained (44.1% selection rate)

**Step 3 — Scale Administration:**
- 5-point Likert scale (1 = "Not at all Consistent"; 5 = "Completely Consistent")
- Administered to 233 mothers

**Step 4 — Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA):**
- Software: SPSS 25.0
- KMO test: 0.903; Bartlett's Test: p < 0.001
- Extraction: Principal component analysis; Rotation: Varimax
- Factor criteria: Eigenvalues > 1, loadings > 0.6, cross-loading differences > 0.05, ≥3 items per factor
- Initial extraction: 10 factors, 60.86% cumulative variance
- Items deleted: 32 (loadings <0.6 or insufficient items per factor)
- **Final structure: 13 items across 3 factors**

**Final EFA Results:**
- KMO = 0.812
- Bartlett χ² = 1069.065, df = 78, p < 0.001
- Cumulative variance: 61.823%

**Step 5 — Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA):**
- Software: AMOS 24.0
- Structural equation modeling; Maximum likelihood estimation

#### Phase 2: Implicit Memory Experiment

**Computerized Protocol (E-Prime 3.0):**
- 13 core statements from Phase 1 (standardized to 7 Chinese characters each)
- 2 randomly highlighted red characters per statement
- 13 distractor sentences

**Three-Phase Experiment:**
1. **Sentence Learning Phase:** Distractor task (judge red character stroke count); unconscious memorization of target statements
2. **Memory Interference Phase:** Count random geometric shapes; reduce explicit memory
3. **Sentence Recognition Phase:** Identify previously seen statements from 26 total

**Quality Control:**
- Randomized presentation order
- Practice trials with different materials
- Data exclusion: error rate >30% or reaction times outside ±3 SD

#### Phase 3: Parenting Behavior Assessment

**Instrument:** Maternal Parenting Behavior Questionnaire (Kim, 2011)
- 50 items, 5 dimensions (10 items each):
  - Affection-Rejection
  - Autonomy-Control
  - Democracy-Authoritarian
  - Intentional Neglect-Overprotection
  - Consistency-Inconsistency
- 5-point Likert scale; Chinese version Cronbach's α: 0.805–0.845

### 2.4 Measures

**Phase 1 — Preliminary Screening Questionnaire for Maternal Persona:**
- Content Validity Index (I-CVI): ≥0.75 for all items
- Scale-Level Content Validity Index (S-CVI/Ave): 0.89

**Phase 2 — Implicit Memory Experiment System (E-Prime 3.0):**
- Key indicators: J ratio (recall accuracy), reaction time (milliseconds)

**Phase 3 — Maternal Parenting Behavior Questionnaire (Kim, 2011):**
- 50 items, 5 dimensions; Cronbach's α = 0.805–0.845

### 2.5 Data Analysis

- EFA & descriptive statistics: SPSS 25.0
- CFA & structural equation modeling: AMOS 24.0
- Inferential statistics: One-way ANOVA
- Effect sizes: η² and Cohen's d
- Significance threshold: α = 0.05

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Factor Structure

The three-dimensional model aligns with Jacobi's persona theory:

**Collective Ideals:** Reflects traditional Chinese cultural emphasis on family as society's basic unit. Women's self-worth traditionally linked to family relationships.

**Individual Ideals:** Includes cultural literacy, self-confidence, self-esteem, communication ability, independence. High self-confidence mothers handle parenting better, fostering positive outcomes.

**Physical/Mental Qualities:** Mothers' physical/mental health significantly impacts parenting effectiveness. Optimistic mothers better manage stress, fostering positive relationships.

### 4.2 Conscious vs. Unconscious Prioritization

*Questionnaire level rankings:* Collective ideals > Individual ideals > Physical/mental qualities

*Implicit memory rankings:* Physical/mental qualities > Collective ideals > Individual ideals

**Interpretation:** People consciously prioritize collective ideals due to social identity and belonging needs. Physical/mental qualities, crucial for survival and reproduction, are prioritized unconsciously despite lower conscious ranking.

**Balance Theory:** "Such balance is essential to a healthy personality mask. If a mother tends toward collective ideals and neglects individual ideals and physical and mental health, she may over-adapt to society." Over-adaptation can lead to mothers losing themselves. Neglecting physical/mental health may develop neurosis and psychosomatic illness.

### 4.3 Persona-Parenting Behavior Relationships

**Overall:** Parenting behaviors differed significantly across personality mask dimensions (η² = 0.123, moderate effect).

**Pattern Summary:**
- **Collective Ideals** mothers → more affectionate, more consistent, less democratic
- **Individual Ideals** mothers → more autonomy-supporting, more democratic, less overprotective, more flexible (less consistent)
- **Physical/Mental Qualities** mothers → more controlling, less affectionate, least democratic

## 5. Implications

### Theoretical
Three-component maternal persona aligns with Jacobi's theory. Conscious and unconscious prioritization differ systematically — a novel finding for parenting research.

### Practical

- **Collective Ideals Mothers:** Balance group interests with individual needs; respect each child's uniqueness
- **Individual Ideals Mothers:** Avoid excessive control; respect autonomy and provide freedom
- **Physical/Mental Qualities Mothers:** Meet children's emotional needs with attention and affection; reduce excessive control

### Intervention Recommendations
Enhanced education and support for mothers:
- Home visits
- Parenting courses
- Psychological counseling

---

## 6. Limitations

1. **Limited Generalizability:** Sample from Region Z only; high cultural homogeneity
2. **Ecological Validity:** Artificial experimental environment fails to capture unconscious processes in real-world scenarios
3. **Scale Development:** Item response theory (IRT) not applied
4. **Cross-Sectional Design:** Cannot identify causal relationships
5. **No Control Group:** No comparison with non-mothers or fathers
6. **Statistical Limitations:** Multiple comparisons without Type I error correction; no multivariate analysis; small effect sizes may reflect cultural sample homogeneity

### Future Directions
- Expand sample representativeness; cross-cultural studies
- Incorporate behavioral observation
- Include experimental control groups
- Longitudinal design
- Structural equation modeling (SEM)
