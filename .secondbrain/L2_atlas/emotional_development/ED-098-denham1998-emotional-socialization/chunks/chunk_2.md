The mechanism:
Emotional competence → Positive peer interactions → More learning opportunities → Better academic outcomes

Children who cannot regulate emotions in classroom settings:
- Cannot sustain attention on academic tasks
- Have more peer conflicts, reducing learning time
- Receive more negative teacher attention
- Develop negative academic self-concept

=== TEMPERAMENT × SOCIALIZATION INTERACTION ===

Denham emphasizes that emotional development reflects BOTH temperament and socialization:

High-reactive temperament (intense emotions):
- Requires MORE responsive emotion coaching, not less
- Parents' natural tendency: withdraw or become punitive (increasing dysregulation)
- Optimal parenting: Calm consistency + high-warmth + clear limits
- These children benefit MOST from emotion coaching interventions

Low-reactive temperament (mild emotions):
- May develop without explicit emotion coaching
- However, still benefits from emotion vocabulary building
- Risk: Emotional awareness may be less developed if never challenged

Key implication: Temperamentally challenging children (like many toddlers with intense emotional profiles) are exactly the children who need emotion coaching most — but also the children most likely to elicit unsupportive parental responses.

=== CULTURAL CONSIDERATIONS ===

Denham acknowledges cultural variation in emotional socialization:
- Display rules vary significantly by culture
- Collectivist cultures may emphasize suppression of emotions that disrupt group harmony
- Individualist cultures may more readily encourage emotional expressiveness
- However: The fundamental mechanisms of emotional socialization (modeling, contingency, teaching) operate cross-culturally
- Basic emotional competence (recognizing and understanding emotions) is a cross-cultural developmental universal

=== IMPLICATIONS FOR INTERVENTION ===

Based on this research framework, effective emotion socialization interventions for young children should:
1. Target parental meta-emotion philosophy (comfort with emotions)
2. Train parents in supportive contingent responding (emotion coaching techniques)
3. Increase parent-child emotion discourse (explicit emotion talk)
4. Address temperament × parenting fit
5. Begin in early childhood (ages 2-5 represent the sensitive period)
