Hostinar, Johnson & Gunnar (2014). Developmental Science. PMC4270957. N=81 (40 children 9-10y, 41 adolescents 15-16y).

Study design: TSST social-evaluative stressor. 3 conditions: parental support / stranger support / alone. Cortisol salivary samples across 7 timepoints.

Key findings:
- Children (9-10y) with parental support: significantly dampened cortisol vs. stranger support (linear β=.16, SE=.06, t=2.75, p=.007; quadratic β=-.04, p=.01)
- Adolescents (15-16y): NO buffering effect from parent vs. stranger (p>.65)
- Age × Condition interaction: F(1,216)=4.88, p=.03 (linear); F(1,158)=4.2, p=.04 (quadratic)
- Adolescents had higher baseline cortisol than children: F(1,86.3)=11.83, p<.001
- Parent-child relationship quality (negativity) did NOT explain the age difference (p>.32)

Mechanism: Developmental shift likely reflects pubertal neurobiological changes or peer-oriented social reorientation, not relationship quality decline.

Key concept: "부모 사회적 완충은 아동기(9-10세)에 강력하게 작동, 청소년기(15-16세)에는 소실 → 유아~아동기가 부모 존재감의 황금기"
