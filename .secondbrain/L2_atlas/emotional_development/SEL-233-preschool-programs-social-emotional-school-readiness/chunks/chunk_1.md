=== OVERVIEW ===

Parents play a central role in supporting early learning that positions young children for success in formal schooling. This review focuses on universal preschool-based outreach strategies helping parents support growth in child social-emotional and self-regulation competencies. Recent research has expanded understanding of neurodevelopmental processes underlying school readiness and parenting's impact on those processes. These insights have fueled innovation in preschool efforts to partner with and support parents. New approaches to intervention design and delivery are emerging to address challenges of reaching and engaging families, especially those from diverse racial, ethnic, cultural, and socioeconomic backgrounds.

=== THE DEVELOPMENT OF SOCIAL-EMOTIONAL COMPETENCIES IN EARLY CHILDHOOD ===

Normatively, child social-emotional learning is rapid and expansive during the preschool and kindergarten years (ages 3-6 years), creating a foundation for future school success. Fundamental advances occur in children's:
- Social skills (collaborate, form friendships, manage conflicts)
- Emotional understanding (recognize and label a range of emotions)
- Self-regulation (control behavioral impulses, modulate emotional arousal)
- Approaches to learning: executive function skills (attention control, working memory, flexible goal-oriented problem-solving)

Concurrent and predictive associations:
- Kindergarten teacher ratings of child social-emotional competencies significantly predicted outcomes TWO DECADES LATER at age 25
- Including: educational success (high school graduation, less grade retention), productive employment, reduced criminal activities
- Social-emotional competencies → positive peer and teacher relationships, productive classroom participation, reduced disruptive behavior

=== PARENTING CONTEXTS AFFECTING SOCIAL-EMOTIONAL LEARNING ===

Two broad domains of parenting practices make fundamental contributions:

1. SOCIOEMOTIONAL SUPPORT
- Warm, sensitive responding
- Avoidance of critical and punitive control
- Contingent, appropriate responding to child needs
- Linked to growth in social-emotional competencies
- Critical and harsh parental control → poor behavioral self-regulation

2. INSTRUCTIONAL SUPPORT (SCAFFOLDING)
- Expose children to multiple learning opportunities
- Foster language development with enriched conversations
- Support autonomous learning and problem-solving
- Provide sensitive-responsive scaffolding to enhance learning motivation and skill acquisition
- Vygotsky's ZPD framework: working just above the child's current level

ADVERSITY CONTEXT:
- Chronic stress erodes positive parenting
- Children in adversity often show delays in social-emotional and self-regulation skills
- Supporting parents with strength-based interventions can reduce school readiness gap

=== PARENT ENGAGEMENT PROGRAMS (RCT EVIDENCE) ===

BEHAVIOR MANAGEMENT FOCUS:
- Incredible Years (IY) in Head Start: improved parenting practices + child social competence
- Chicago Parent Program: family routines + stress management + parent-child problem-solving
- ParentCorps: behavior management + emotions + problem-solving + cultural strengths
  → RCT: improved parenting + child behavior; sustained effects on mental health AND academic performance 3 years later

SENSITIVE-RESPONSIVE PARENTING FOCUS:
- Play and Learning Strategies (PALS): 19 home visits, warm/sensitive/responsive parenting + scaffold attention
  → RCT: greater improvements in parenting; children gained social, self-regulation, and language skills
- Circle of Security in Head Start: 10-week video-based program
  → Children showed greater improvement in executive function (inhibitory control)

PARENT-CHILD COMMUNICATION AND GUIDED LEARNING:
- REDI-P: activity kits with storybooks + conversation games highlighting SEL; home visitor coaching
  → RCT: improved parent-child communication + child school readiness; sustained benefits through FIFTH GRADE
- Getting Ready for School (GRS): 8-week workshop on academic + self-regulation skills
  → Enhanced child math skills; higher attendance → increased gains in academic AND self-regulation

=== CHALLENGES OF PARENT ENGAGEMENT ===

- Most ECE parent outreach programs enroll only 10-34% of eligible parents
- Of enrollees, most attend fewer than 50% of sessions
- Practical barriers: work conflicts (50%), night classes (11%), transportation (14%), too tired (12%)

SUCCESSFUL ENGAGEMENT STRATEGIES:
- Getting Ready: coach educators on 8 engagement strategies (communicate, affirm competencies, mutual decisions, model practices)
- Family Check-Up (FCU): motivational interviewing + family strengths assessment → 87% engagement
- REDI-P with personalized invitations + text reminders: 38% vs 53% attendance improvement

TECHNOLOGY-BASED DELIVERY:
- Virtual PALS: replicated in-person findings for parenting skills + child self-regulation
- FCU Online: videos + graphics + interactive activities + text reminders
- Human-centered design (HCD) for activity kits: guided kits used more, rated more positively
  → For parents with less formal education: guided kits promoted significant gains in scaffolding ability

=== KEY FINDINGS ===

1. Programs that provided INTENSIVE COACHING in positive parenting + HOME LEARNING ACTIVITIES produced strongest gains
2. "Fully developed" programs targeting parents (not just ECE center) = measurably better outcomes
3. Kindergarten social-emotional competencies predict life outcomes at age 25
4. Technology and telehealth delivery can match in-person outcomes
5. Motivational interviewing + family strengths approach → high engagement (87%)
6. Scaffolding + parental warmth/sensitivity = the two foundational components
