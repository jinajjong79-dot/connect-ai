Smith-Nielsen et al. (2024). PLoS ONE. PMC10830029. N=99 Danish mother-child dyads, child age 4.5y (mean).

Study design: Parental mentalizing measured offline via PRFQ-IC (Parental Reflective Functioning Questionnaire – Infant/Child). Online mental state language coded from play interaction video. Child pretend play complexity measured via standardized observation.

Key findings:
- Parental offline mentalizing → mental state language (online, b=3.44, p=.008, R²=.08)
- Mental state language → child pretend play complexity (b=0.11, p=.002, R²=.15)
- Mediation: indirect effect b=0.37, BCa 95% CI [0.10; 0.76] (significant)
- Offline mentalizing alone → pretend play: NOT significant (b=0.35, p=.42)
- Conclusion: Parental mentalizing must translate into actual mental state language during play to affect child pretend play complexity. Offline capacity alone is insufficient.

Clinical implication: Parenting interventions should target in-the-moment mental state verbalization during joint play, not just parental insight/awareness.

Key concept: "Offline mentalizing alone not significant — must translate to action (online mental state language)"
