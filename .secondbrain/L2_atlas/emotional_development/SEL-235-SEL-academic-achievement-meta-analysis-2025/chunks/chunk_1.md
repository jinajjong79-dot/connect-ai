Social-Emotional Learning and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analysis (2025 Update)
Source: PMC12649258 | 22 RCTs, N=24,510 | Elementary and Middle School Students
ATLAS ID: SEL-235
Category: emotional_development / social-emotional learning / academic achievement
Tier: 2 | Grade: A
Year: 2025

=== OVERVIEW ===

This meta-analysis examined 22 studies involving 24,510 elementary and middle school students (published 2011-2021) to assess how social-emotional learning (SEL) programs affect academic performance.

=== KEY FINDINGS ===

Overall Effects:
- SEL interventions produced positive effects on overall academic achievement (g = 0.08)
- Mathematics: g = 0.08
- English language arts: g = 0.07
- Science: g = 0.06
- GPA: g = 0.33 (strongest effect)

Critical Moderators:
- Study design: Quasi-experimental designs yielded significantly larger effects than randomized controlled trials (RCTs), particularly for elementary students and English language arts outcomes
- Student SES and report type (peer-reviewed vs. reports) showed no significant moderating effects

Evidence Base Limitation:
- Evidence base was "dominated by elementary school research (20 studies), with findings for middle school students derived from only two studies"
- More studies on middle school students and science performance needed

=== METHODS ===

- Systematic review covering SEL RCTs published 2011-2021
- 22 studies, N=24,510 students
- Elementary and middle school settings
- Academic outcomes: mathematics, ELA, science, GPA
- Moderator analyses: study design, SES, grade level, report type

=== SIGNIFICANCE ===

This 2025 update confirms that SEL continues to show positive (though modest) academic effects. The GPA effect (g=0.33) is substantially larger than subject-specific effects, suggesting SEL may operate through motivation, engagement, and persistence rather than direct content knowledge transfer.
