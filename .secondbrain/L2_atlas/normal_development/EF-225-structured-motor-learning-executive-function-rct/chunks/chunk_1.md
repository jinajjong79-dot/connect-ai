Title: Structured Motor Learning and Executive Functions in Preschool Children: A Randomized Controlled Trial
PMC ID: PMC12104419
Year: 2025
Tier: 2 | Grade: A
Category: normal_development
Keywords: structured motor learning, executive function, preschool, RCT, working memory, physical activity, motor skills, ages 4-6, cognitive development

---

ABSTRACT

RCT examining how 12 weeks of structured motor learning activities affect executive functions in preschool children (ages 4-6) compared to outdoor free play. N=80 (intervention n=36, control n=44). 12 weeks, 2×30-minute sessions/week. Significant working memory improvement; inhibitory control and cognitive flexibility not significant (duration insufficient).

Core finding: "N=80. 8주 운동 프로그램→작업기억↑ (억제/유연성은 기간 부족). 몸을 쓰면 뇌가 자란다"

INTRODUCTION

The physical-cognitive connection:
Growing evidence links physical activity to cognitive development in children:
- Exercise increases BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) → neural growth
- Motor skill acquisition activates cerebellum and prefrontal cortex simultaneously
- Movement-based learning integrates body and mind
- "몸을 쓰면 뇌가 자란다" — physical movement and EF share neural substrates

But not all physical activity is equal:
- Free play: high physical activity but low cognitive demand
- Structured motor learning: physical activity + rule following + sequence memory + attention = EF-loaded

What makes motor learning structured vs. free:
Structured motor learning involves:
- Teacher-guided instruction with explicit learning goals
- Progressive skill difficulty
- Environmental constraints (modified task demands)
- Reflective components (discuss what was learned)
This structure adds cognitive load to physical activity — turning exercise into EF training.

Theoretical frameworks applied:
1. Constructivism: Children actively construct knowledge through movement experience
2. Theory of Constraints (Newell): Modify environment, individual, and task constraints to optimize learning → creates optimal challenge zone for cognitive engagement

METHODS

Design: Randomized controlled trial
Sample: N=80 preschool children, ages 4-6
Groups: Intervention (n=36), Control (n=44)
Comparison condition: Outdoor FREE play (not sedentary control)
Duration: 12 weeks
Frequency: 2 sessions per week
Session length: 30 minutes each

Intervention session structure (30 minutes):
Phase 1 — Game Scenarios (5 min):
- Teacher introduces a problem or challenge using story/game frame
- "We're going to be athletes training for the Olympics today"
- Sets motivation and attentional focus

Phase 2 — Fundamental Motor Skills Acquisition (10 min):
- Targeted motor skill with explicit instruction
- Modified constraints to create challenge
- Examples: catching with non-dominant hand, balancing on modified surface, throwing to moving target
- Teacher provides feedback and scaffolding

Phase 3 — Active Exploration and Creative Expression (10 min):
- Child-initiated application of learned skills in novel contexts
- Less teacher direction, more child creativity
- Integration of skills into play scenarios

Phase 4 — Reflection and Appreciation (5 min):
- Brief group discussion: "What did we learn? What was hard?"
- Self-regulation of attention and metacognition
- Celebration of effort and improvement

Control condition: Standard outdoor free play
- Same duration and frequency
- Unstructured outdoor recess
- Teacher present but not guiding EF-relevant activities

EF Assessment Tool: EF-TOUCH computerized battery
- Working Memory: spatial span and updating tasks
- Inhibitory Control: go/no-go and conflict inhibition tasks
- Cognitive Flexibility: task-switching paradigm

Analysis: Multilevel regression controlling for baseline scores, age, and gender

RESULTS

Working Memory:
- Significant improvement: B = 0.20, p < 0.01
- Direction: Intervention > Control after 12 weeks
- "The intervention group demonstrated significant improvements in working memory"
- Mechanism: Motor learning requires holding sequences in mind (temporal WM) and spatial positions (spatial WM)

Inhibitory Control:
- NOT significant: B = -0.07, p = 0.15
- No meaningful difference between groups
- Possible explanations: 12 weeks insufficient for IC effects; free play also develops IC to some degree

Cognitive Flexibility:
- NOT significant: B = -0.03, p = 0.23
- No meaningful difference between groups
- CF requires longer training periods; may need more EF-explicit instruction beyond motor context

DISCUSSION

Why working memory responds to motor learning:

Motor learning IS a working memory task:
- Learning a new motor sequence: hold step 1, execute step 2, remember feedback from step 1
- Spatial positioning: hold target location in mind while executing movement
- Teacher instructions: hold multi-step instructions while executing movements
- Each session provides 30 minutes of WM training through physical action

The embodied cognition perspective:
Motor learning and WM share neural infrastructure:
- Cerebellum: timing, sequencing — critical for both motor coordination and WM updating
- PFC: holds goals and instructions — active during both motor learning and WM tasks
- Physical movement generates proprioceptive feedback that anchors cognitive representations

Why IC and CF didn't change — but might with more time:
- 12 weeks × 2 sessions = 24 sessions (vs. EF-224's 12 sessions of EXPLICIT EF training)
- Motor learning indirectly trains EF; direct EF training may be more efficient for IC and CF
- Authors recommend: "increased frequency, duration, and intensity for enhanced outcomes"
- Hybrid approach: structured motor learning + explicit EF games (EF-224 protocol) likely optimal

Comparison with outdoor free play:
- Control condition (free play) showed NO significant EF gains
- "Not all outdoor physical activity develops EF" — structure matters
- This directly counters the assumption that outdoor play automatically develops cognition

Korean context:
- Many Korean preschool PE programs are structured (formal sports-based)
- But the structure in this study is cognitive-movement integrated, not just drill-based sports
- Adding Phase 4 (reflection) and Phase 1 (scenario framing) to existing PE transforms it
- Cost: near-zero (modify existing PE time, add reflection component)

CONCLUSION

12 weeks of structured motor learning (2×30 min/week) significantly improved working memory in preschoolers compared to free outdoor play. Inhibitory control and cognitive flexibility were not significantly affected — likely due to duration limitations or the need for more explicit EF-targeted instruction. Structured motor learning is a feasible, low-cost approach to EF enhancement through physical education.

Key insight: "운동+인지를 통합한 구조화된 신체활동이 자유놀이보다 실행기능에 더 효과적이다"

Optimal prescription: Combine structured motor learning (EF-225) with explicit EF play training (EF-224) for comprehensive EF development targeting all three components.
