Title: Developmental Trajectories of Executive Functions from Preschool to Kindergarten
PMC ID: PMC9296695
Year: 2022
Tier: 2 | Grade: A
Category: normal_development
Keywords: executive function, longitudinal, developmental trajectory, preschool, kindergarten, inhibitory control, working memory, cognitive flexibility, latent growth curve

---

ABSTRACT

This longitudinal research examined EF development across the preschool-to-kindergarten transition using 5 measurement points. N=758 children (49.4% female) from 104 preschool classrooms. Main findings: EF functions best as a unitary construct at this age; growth is nonlinear with most occurring in the preschool year; lower-EF children show accelerated kindergarten growth (convergence effect).

Core finding: "3~5세 실행기능 급성장기. 억제통제가 먼저, 인지유연성이 나중."

INTRODUCTION

Why study EF trajectories:
- EF is not static — it develops dramatically in early childhood
- Understanding WHEN growth occurs informs WHEN to intervene
- The preschool-to-kindergarten transition is particularly critical
- Low-income children often show EF lags that are addressable with targeted programs

Three EF components:
1. Inhibitory Control (IC): suppressing prepotent responses, resisting distraction
   - Develops earliest — even toddlers show rudimentary IC
   - Rapid growth: ages 3-5
2. Working Memory (WM): holding and updating information in mind
   - Develops through preschool and continues into school age
   - Strongly predicts language and academic outcomes (EF-223)
3. Cognitive Flexibility (CF): shifting attention between rules or perspectives
   - Develops latest of the three
   - Requires IC and WM as prerequisites
   - Emerges more clearly at ages 5-7
   - "인지유연성이 나중" = last to develop, most complex

METHODS

Design: Longitudinal, 5 measurement points
Sample: N=758 children, 49.4% female
Demographics: 48.8% Black/African American, 21.9% white, 12.5% Hispanic/Latino
Socioeconomic: Average family income 145% of federal poverty level (low-income)
Setting: 104 preschool classrooms in two southeastern U.S. districts
Measurement points: 5 (fall preschool → spring preschool → fall K → winter K → spring K)

EF Tasks administered:
- Inhibitory Control: Head-Toes-Knees-Shoulders task (motor inhibition)
- Working Memory: Mr. Peanut task (spatial updating)
- Cognitive Flexibility: Dimensional Change Card Sort (DCCS) — switching rules

Statistical approach:
- Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA): testing unitary vs. 3-factor structure
- Latent Growth Curve Modeling (LGC): modeling growth trajectories
- Measurement invariance testing across time points

RESULTS

Structure of EF (CFA):
- EF functioned best as a "single unitary construct" (one general factor)
- Separate component structure did not fit better than unitary model
- Conclusion: at preschool age, EF components are highly correlated and largely develop together
- "억제통제가 먼저, 인지유연성이 나중" is a developmental sequence but they are interrelated

Growth Trajectories (LGC):

Overall growth pattern:
- "Nonlinear, with disproportionately more growth during the preschool year"
- Preschool year: RAPID growth (steep slope)
- Kindergarten year: SLOWER growth (flatter slope)
- Implication: Preschool is the prime window for EF development

Initial ability × growth interaction:
- Children with HIGHER initial EF showed STEEPER preschool growth (Matthew effect)
- Rich get richer: early advantage accelerates further in preschool

Convergence effect (key finding):
- Children with LOWER initial EF showed ACCELERATED growth in kindergarten
- By spring kindergarten, EF gap between initially high and low groups substantially reduced
- "Convergence in EF by the end of kindergarten"
- Implication: Kindergarten may be a recovery window for children who enter with EF lags

EF developmental milestones derived:
| Age | EF Development Status |
|-----|----------------------|
| 3 years | Basic IC emerging; WM very limited; CF absent |
| 3.5-4 years | IC rapid growth; WM beginning; CF early signs |
| 4-5 years | IC more reliable; WM expanding; CF emerging |
| 5-6 (K entry) | IC good; WM moderate; CF inconsistent |
| End of kindergarten | All three components more reliable; convergence across children |

DISCUSSION

The preschool sensitive window:
The most rapid EF growth occurs in preschool (ages 3-5). This is the optimal intervention window. Programs targeting EF (Tools of the Mind, play-based curricula) should start at age 3-4 for maximum impact.

The kindergarten recovery window:
Children who enter kindergarten with low EF are NOT destined to stay behind — the convergence effect shows they show accelerated growth in kindergarten. This suggests:
- Quality kindergarten instruction can partially compensate for early EF delays
- EF interventions in kindergarten remain meaningful
- But preschool prevention is better than kindergarten remediation

Why IC develops before CF:
Developmental sequence hypothesis:
1. IC: requires only STOPPING a response (one cognitive operation)
2. WM: requires HOLDING information while processing (two operations)
3. CF: requires IC + WM + SWITCHING (three operations)
Each builds on the previous. This is why you cannot "teach" CF to a 3-year-old who hasn't developed IC yet.

For parenting guidance:
- Age 3: work on IC (simple rules, turn-taking, "stop" games)
- Age 4-5: WM games (memory games, sequences, following multi-step instructions)
- Age 5-6: CF games (sorting by different rules, understanding multiple perspectives)
- "아이의 실행기능 발달 단계에 맞는 놀이를 선택해야 한다"

Low-income children context:
Sample was predominantly low-income — findings suggest even disadvantaged children show substantial EF growth. The convergence effect is hopeful: appropriate early education can reduce EF gaps.

CONCLUSION

EF develops as a largely unified system from preschool to kindergarten, with the fastest growth occurring in the preschool year. Inhibitory control develops before cognitive flexibility. Lower-EF children show accelerated kindergarten growth (convergence). Preschool is the optimal window for EF interventions; kindergarten offers secondary recovery opportunity.

Key message: "3~5세는 실행기능이 가장 빠르게 자라는 시기입니다. 이 창(window)을 활용하는 것이 중요합니다."
