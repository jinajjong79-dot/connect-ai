=== OVERVIEW ===

This review article examines the critical but underexplored roles of parent self-regulation (PSR) and parent-child coregulation in disciplinary practices. The authors emphasize: (1) incorporating multiple PSR domains to understand their respective and interactive contributions, and (2) examining how PSR interfaces with parent-child coregulation to explain pathways linking harsh discipline to child maladjustment.

Global Scale: Approximately 1 billion children globally are disciplined harshly every day, despite established harms.

=== KEY FINDINGS ===

Parental Emotion Regulation:
- Higher emotional reactivity in parents → greater child abuse potential and harsher parenting
- About half of mothers reported anger regarding child misbehavior increased spanking likelihood
- Harsh parents demonstrate heightened attention bias toward negative emotions and threat
- Cognitive-emotional coupling: negative interpretation bias → harsh discipline cycle

Parental Cognitive Regulation (Executive Function):
- Lower EF is directly associated with harsh discipline
- Harsher parents: rigid cognitive schemas, reduced problem-solving flexibility
- Greater attention to negative vs. positive child behaviors
- Domains: working memory, planning capacity, mental flexibility
- Without these capacities → discipline becomes reactive rather than reflective

Parental Biological Regulation:
- Harsh parents: dysregulated stress response (hyperarousal or hypoarousal)
- Higher baseline cortisol and greater autonomic reactivity
- Curvilinear relationship between biological reactivity and harsh discipline severity
- Encompasses: sympathetic/parasympathetic NS functioning, cortisol reactivity, cardiovascular responsiveness

Parent-Child Coregulation Dynamics (핵심):
- Coercive cycle: child noncompliance → parental negativity escalation → maladaptive pattern reinforcement
- Dysfunctional contingencies strengthen through noncompliance-avoidance coupling
- Emotional rigidity or indiscriminate affect characterizes harsh parent dyads
- Weaker biological synchrony observed in abuse contexts
- In functional relationships: bidirectional regulation where parents help children modulate responses, and children's behavior reinforces parents' regulatory capacity
- In coercive cycles: this bidirectional process becomes adversarial

=== PSR THREE-DOMAIN MODEL ===

Emotional Domain: emotion recognition, emotional reactivity, emotion regulation strategies
Cognitive Domain: executive function (working memory, planning, flexibility), attention bias, cognitive schemas
Biological Domain: autonomic nervous system regulation, HPA axis (cortisol), stress reactivity

Critical insight: Most interventions address single PSR domains → modest benefits. Combined approaches targeting multiple regulatory systems → stronger effect sizes.

=== INTERVENTION IMPLICATIONS ===

Existing Programs:
- Triple-P: shows effectiveness when targeting regulatory processes
- Parent Management Training: effective for regulatory processes
- Gap: most interventions target single PSR domains, not integrated approaches

What's needed:
- Interventions explicitly addressing coregulation patterns
- Multiple-domain simultaneous targeting
- Real-time monitoring of parent regulatory states during discipline moments

=== ATLAS SIGNIFICANCE ===

This paper provides the theoretical foundation for "부모가 자기조절 안 되면 가혹한 훈육으로 간다":
- Parent EF↓ → harsh discipline↑ (cognitive regulation domain)
- Parent emotional reactivity↑ → abuse potential↑
- Coregulation is the mechanism: not just what parents do TO children, but the MUTUAL regulation process

Clinical direction:
- Adding PSR training to existing parent education programs
- Building coregulation awareness: "나는 아이와 함께 감정을 조절하고 있는가?"
- Harsh discipline = regulatory failure, not a disciplinary choice
