# Maternal Responsive Parenting Trajectories From Birth to Age 3 and Children's Self-Esteem at First Grade

**Source:** Frontiers in Psychology, Volume 13, 2022. DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.870669
**Journal:** Front Psychol. 2022 Apr 28;13:870669
**PMC:** PMC9096869
**Data:** Panel Study on Korean Children (한국아동패널)

## Abstract

This paper examines the quality and stability of responsive parenting practices of mothers with infants and longitudinal links between these practices and children's self-esteem. Using data from the Panel Study on Korean Children, this study identified Korean mothers' responsive parenting trajectories from birth to age three and examined their associations with children's self-esteem at first grade.

Korean mothers developed one of three responsive parenting patterns from birth to age three:
- **Low trajectory** (19.0%): consistently low responsiveness
- **Moderate trajectory** (66.0%): moderate responsiveness (majority group)
- **High trajectory** (15.0%): consistently high responsiveness

Children's self-esteem differed according to their mother's responsive parenting trajectory. First-graders with mothers displaying the low responsive parenting trajectory were more likely to have lower self-esteem than children of mothers with moderate or high trajectories.

## Key Findings

- Longitudinal link confirmed: mother-reported responsive parenting during infancy predicts child-reported self-esteem at first grade
- Early responsive parenting (birth–age 3) is a significant predictor of self-esteem in later developmental stages
- 66% of Korean mothers showed moderate responsiveness — the largest group
- Only 15% maintained high responsiveness throughout infancy to age 3
- Self-esteem in first grade varies significantly by parenting trajectory group

## Implications for Parent Communication

- Responsive parenting interventions should begin as early as possible (birth to 36 months)
- Parenting education should address consistency of responsiveness, not just intensity
- Low-trajectory mothers (19%) represent highest-risk group requiring targeted support
- Improving from low to moderate responsiveness can meaningfully improve child self-esteem outcomes
- Korean Children Panel Study data confirms these findings are directly applicable to Korean families
