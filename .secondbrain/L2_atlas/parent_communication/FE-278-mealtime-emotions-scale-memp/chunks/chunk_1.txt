White et al. (2022). Maternal & Child Nutrition. PMC9218312. N=246 (Section 1, EFA); N=186 (Section 2, validation). Child ages 1.5-6 years. Scale development and validation study using exploratory factor analysis (EFA).

Scale developed: MEM-P (Mealtime Emotions Measure for Parents)

Three-factor structure:
1. MEM-P Efficacy (positive emotions, 41.1% variance): prepared, comfortable, emotionally controlled, confident managing child distress → POSITIVE
2. MEM-P Anxiety (negative, 14.2% variance): nervous, emotionally confused, distressed, embarrassed
3. MEM-P Stress and Anger (negative, 7.3% variance): stress, frustration, anger (low relaxation/happiness)

Relationships to feeding practices (controlling for general anxiety/depression):
- HIGH Efficacy → child involvement↑ + healthy home food environment + nutrition modeling↑
- HIGH Anxiety → allowing more child control over eating + reduced parental involvement
- HIGH Stress/Anger → "greater use of food to regulate emotions" (coercive feeding, 강압적 수유)

Main conclusion: Parental mealtime emotions are distinct from general psychopathology and meaningfully predict food parenting practices. Mealtime efficacy = proximal target for intervention.
