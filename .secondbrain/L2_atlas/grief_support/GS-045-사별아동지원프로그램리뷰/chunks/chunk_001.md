# When a Parent Dies — Systematic Review of Bereavement Support Programs

**Authors:** Ann-Sofie Bergman, Ulf Axberg, Elizabeth Hanson
**Journal:** BMC Palliative Care, 2017 (16:39)
**PMC:** PMC5553589

---

## Abstract

Systematic review of 17 empirical studies evaluating support interventions for parentally bereaved children (ages 0–18). Key conclusion: **"relatively brief interventions can prevent children from developing more severe problems after the loss of a parent, such as traumatic grief and mental health problems."**

---

## Prevalence & Risk

- ~3–4% of children in developed nations lose a parent before age 18
- Elevated risk of: mental health problems, anxiety, depression, somatic symptoms
- Scandinavian studies: elevated mortality and suicide risk in childhood/early adulthood
- Surviving parent's own grief limits capacity to support children

---

## Study Design

- **Databases searched**: PubMed, PsycINFO, Cinahl, PILOTS, ProQuest Sociology
- **Total screened**: 1,706 abstracts → **17 studies** met criteria
- **Study quality**: 13 strong, 3 fairly strong, 1 weaker evidence
- **Study types**: 15 RCTs, 1 quasi-experimental, 1 pre-post-test

---

## 4 Types of Interventions

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Group for children | Support/processing groups, camp activities |
| Family interventions | Conjoint family therapy sessions |
| Parental guidance | Supporting remaining caregivers |
| Camp-based | Weekend therapeutic camps |

- Duration: 90 min to 1 year; most 6–14 sessions
- **14 of 17 studies** targeted both children AND remaining caregivers

---

## Effect Sizes

### Large Effects
- Children's traumatic grief (writing intervention)
- Parents' feelings of being supported (Family Bereavement Program)

### Medium Effects
- Parental warmth, positive parenting, mental health, grief discussions
- Child: traumatic grief symptoms, health, restlessness
- Parental depression

### Small Effects
- Intrusive grief thoughts, PTSD symptoms, self-esteem
- Anxiety and depression (especially girls)
- Behavior problems, social competence
- Cortisol stress response
- Coping and control beliefs

---

## The Family Bereavement Program (Most Studied)

**Structure**: 14 sessions — separate groups for caregivers + children (8–12, 12–16), plus joint sessions
**Focus**:
- Parent-child relationships
- Positive parenting skills
- Grief coping strategies
- Family communication
- Stability and predictability

**Evidence**: Sustained positive effects over **6-year follow-up**

---

## KEY FINDING: Parent Support → Child Benefit

> "When parents are supported, they can demonstrate an enhanced capacity to support their children."

- Supporting the surviving caregiver is the **primary lever** for child outcomes
- Joint sessions often provided the **first opportunity** for parent-child discussion of loss
- Children avoid expressing grief to protect parents → can be misread as lack of impact

---

## Research Gaps

1. **Age gap**: Interventions mostly for school-age; youngest children most vulnerable but least studied
2. **Geography**: Almost all studies from USA/UK — limited cultural diversity
3. **Follow-up**: Most studies lack long-term assessment
4. **Boys vs girls**: Girls showed more response; boys underrepresented in findings
5. **Physical health**: Few measures of somatic symptoms or school functioning

---

## Conclusions

> "Relatively brief interventions may help prevent children from developing more severe problems, such as mental health problems and traumatic grief after the loss of a parent."

- Brief = 6–14 sessions is sufficient for prevention
- Reassess at program end — some children need more intensive support
- Joint parent-child work is more effective than child-only interventions

---

## Application to Sehyun (세현이) Context

- **세현이 지원 핵심 원칙**: Genmon(아버지)이 지원받는 것이 세현이에게 직접 이익
- 짧은 구조화된 개입(6-14회기)으로 트라우마 슬픔 예방 가능
- 아동이 부모 보호 목적으로 감정 억제 → 별도 공간 제공 필수
- 형제 사별(GS-044)과 부모 지원(GS-045) = 세현이 케어의 두 축
